`<PaneGate actionPlacement>`: the pooled footnote can carry the way out of it.

`action` rendered only in the BLOCKED arm. But a pane a visitor may read and
not write is `available`, and its per-control gates pool their reasons into
one footnote — "sign in to reply", "sign in to react", once each — while the
door out of all of them lived in the one arm that page never renders. Measured
on a live storefront, where the footnote said what was refused and offered
nowhere to go.

`actionPlacement="always"` draws `action` under the pooled sentences too,
inside the same block, because the reasons and the way out are one statement.
Only where there is something to pool: no refusal and no reasons means no
door. The default `"blocked"` is what ships today.
